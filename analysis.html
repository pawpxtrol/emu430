Key takeaways

  • Topic: Street-animal feeding operations across Ankara districts (ilçeler) from July 2024 to June 2025, based on monthly health-directorate operational records.
  • Data: ~3,700+ neighborhood-day records aggregated from 11 monthly workbooks, with feeding-point counts, kg/day of dry food, and vehicle / personnel staffing.
  • Important aspects: District names needed trimming; food / dates / staff fields were text-encoded with inconsistent formats (March 2025 dates as "1.03.2025", vehicles as "-" or the typo "Y3").
  • Interesting results: A handful of districts (notably Çankaya, Keçiören, Mamak) dominate row counts; monthly totals fluctuate, and the kg-per-record distribution is right-skewed with consolidated large drops in the tail.

    distinct_days      = n_distinct(as.Date(tarih)),
    distinct_ilce      = n_distinct(ilce),
    missing_mama       = sum(is.na(mama_kg)),
    missing_arac       = sum(is.na(arac_sayisi)),
    missing_personel   = sum(is.na(personel_sayisi))
  ) |>
  knitr::kable(caption = "Dataset coverage and missingness across all months")

The monthly workbooks do not always report every calendar day, so distinct_days is well below the calendar maximum. We treat that as a reporting feature rather than imputing days.

One row in the October 2024 workbook carries an obvious 2022-10-18 date (a source typo). It stays in feeding for traceability and shows up in the coverage table above, but we exclude it from the time-axis charts below so the monthly axis spans only the actual reporting window (Jul 2024 → Jun 2025).

Operational efficiency: kg per resource

Productivity question: for the same staffing / fleet headcount, which districts move the most food?

Code 
ops <- feeding_ts |>
  filter(!is.na(mama_kg)) |>
  group_by(ilce) |>
  summarise(
    rows           = n(),
    total_kg       = sum(mama_kg, na.rm = TRUE),
    median_staff   = median(personel_sayisi, na.rm = TRUE),
    median_vehicle = median(arac_sayisi,     na.rm = TRUE),
    kg_per_staff   = total_kg / median_staff,
    kg_per_vehicle = total_kg / median_vehicle,
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  arrange(desc(kg_per_staff))

ops |>
  mutate(across(c(total_kg, kg_per_staff, kg_per_vehicle), \(x) round(x, 1))) |>
  knitr::kable(caption = "Annual kg of dry food per staff and per vehicle, by district.")
Code 
ops |>
  mutate(ilce = stats::reorder(factor(ilce), kg_per_staff)) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = ilce, y = kg_per_staff)) +
  geom_col(fill = "#2E86AB") +
  coord_flip() +
  scale_y_continuous(labels = label_number(suffix = " kg", big.mark = ",")) +
  labs(x = NULL, y = "kg / staff member (annual total)") +
  theme_minimal(base_size = 12)

Honest caveat. personel_sayisi and arac_sayisi have only one distinct value across the entire 11-month dataset — i.e. every record was filed with the same fleet/staffing snapshot. That means the ranking in the figure above is identical to a raw total_kg ranking; what changes is only the absolute scale (kg per one unit of resource). With finer-grained daily staff/vehicle counts, the same code would reveal true efficiency gaps — a Yenimahalle-with-9-staff-moving-3kg vs. Keçiören-with-9-staff-moving-200kg comparison.

Weekday vs. weekend, and within-district consistency

Temporal question: does the operation scale down on weekends, and how stable is each district’s daily load?

wday_lbl <- c("Mon", "Tue", "Wed", "Thu", "Fri", "Sat", "Sun")

by_wday <- feeding_ts |>
  filter(!is.na(tarih), !is.na(mama_kg)) |>

feeding_ts |>
  filter(!is.na(mama_kg), ilce %in% big_ilce) |>
  group_by(ilce) |>
  summarise(
    rows     = n(),
    mean_kg  = mean(mama_kg, na.rm = TRUE),
    sd_kg    = sd(mama_kg,   na.rm = TRUE),
    cv       = sd_kg / mean_kg,
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  arrange(desc(cv)) |>
  mutate(across(c(mean_kg, sd_kg, cv), \(x) round(x, 2))) |>
  knitr::kable(caption = "Within-district variability of kg/day. High coefficient of variation (CV) = unstandardised schedule; low CV = routine flat operation.")

A high CV (e.g. sd / mean > 1) suggests big fluctuations from day to day — likely consolidated bulk deliveries rather than a smooth daily schedule. A low CV suggests a routine flat operation.

Conclusions

  • Spatial concentration: A handful of districts (Çankaya, Keçiören, Mamak and a few others) account for most of the reported activity. Tying this to population, urban form, or program emphasis remains a hypothesis without external data.
  • Temporal patterns: Monthly volume is uneven, partly driven by reporting effort rather than real demand. The kg totals are less sensitive to that and offer a more reliable workload proxy.
  • Right-skewed food amounts: A long upper tail represents consolidated bulk feedings at specific points; the median is a better summary than the mean.
  • Productivity (kg per resource): With the published staff/vehicle figures fixed at a single value, the ranking of kg-per-staff and kg-per-vehicle reduces to a rescaled total-kg ranking. That itself is a finding — the source does not publish variable daily staffing, so cross-district efficiency comparisons must wait on richer data.
  • Loading per feeding point: A clear right-skew in kg / feeding point identifies “hot stations” where a single point absorbs disproportionate load — candidates for splitting or route rebalancing.
  • Staff–food relationship: Because reported personel_sayisi is uniform across districts, the staff-vs-kg scatter collapses to a vertical line; the implied “perfectly linear allocation” assumption cannot be validated from the published file.
  • Weekday rhythm: Records and median kg per record are visibly lower on weekends in the weekday charts, suggesting the operation does scale down rather than running flat 7 days a week.
  • Within-district consistency: Coefficient-of-variation across the major districts ranges widely; the highest-CV districts use sporadic bulk drops, while low-CV districts run a flat routine.
  • Data quality matters: Different months use different date formats, and one row carries an obvious 2022 typo — a reminder that reproducible cleaning is part of the analysis, not a side concern.

## Conclusions

- **Spatial concentration:** A handful of districts (Çankaya, Keçiören, Mamak and a few others) account for most of the reported activity. Tying this to population, urban form, or program emphasis remains a **hypothesis** without external data.
- **Temporal patterns:** Monthly volume is uneven, partly driven by reporting effort rather than real demand. The kg totals are less sensitive to that and offer a more reliable workload proxy.
- **Right-skewed food amounts:** A long upper tail represents **consolidated bulk feedings** at specific points; the median is a better summary than the mean.
- **Productivity (kg per resource):** With the published staff/vehicle figures fixed at a single value, the *ranking* of kg-per-staff and kg-per-vehicle reduces to a rescaled total-kg ranking. That itself is a finding — the source does not publish variable daily staffing, so cross-district efficiency comparisons must wait on richer data.
- **Loading per feeding point:** A clear right-skew in **kg / feeding point** identifies "hot stations" where a single point absorbs disproportionate load — candidates for splitting or route rebalancing.
- **Staff–food relationship:** Because reported `personel_sayisi` is uniform across districts, the staff-vs-kg scatter collapses to a vertical line; the implied "perfectly linear allocation" assumption cannot be validated from the published file.
- **Weekday rhythm:** Records and median kg per record are visibly **lower on weekends** in the weekday charts, suggesting the operation does scale down rather than running flat 7 days a week.
- **Within-district consistency:** Coefficient-of-variation across the major districts ranges widely; the highest-CV districts use sporadic bulk drops, while low-CV districts run a flat routine.
- **Data quality matters:** Different months use different date formats, and one row carries an obvious 2022 typo — a reminder that reproducible cleaning is part of the analysis, not a side concern.
